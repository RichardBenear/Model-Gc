# Independent review of EX-GC57 and EX-GC58

**GC57 resolves the light-drift discrepancy and identifies a missing coupling to intrinsic spin. GC58 supplies that coupling through local rotations on lattice squares and uses the same interaction to calculate the field sourced by spin.** This is progress toward reciprocal frame dragging. The rule for propagating the shift field, its source strength and the normalization of the new spin coupling remain inputs.

## GC57 — A clean light benchmark and a qualified rotation result

The one-way light launch resolves GC56’s discrepancy prospectively. Across two central momenta and three widths, matching each detector to its own spectral weighting gives an energy-centroid error below 1.4 × 10⁻⁵ and an envelope-centroid error below 3 × 10⁻⁵. The old launch also reproduces the old miss. This establishes common drift in the uniform-shift lattice benchmark. [GC57 plan](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/spec/EX-GC57_plan.md) · [GC57 results](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/results/EX-GC57/EX-GC57_note.md)

The rotating-field experiment is less settled. The orbital energy splitting and torque give consistent frequency estimates, and reversal of the source current reverses the orbital signal. The registered joint light–orbit comparison P2 and the dipole-pattern prediction P3 fail. The note’s opening count should read **two failed predictions and one failed check**: C3 is the separate small-clock calibration, which reads 4.5% high. Post hoc, a finite-source continuum calculation explains the axis-to-plane field ratio (−1.565 versus −1.561 on the lattice), and averaging the field over each probe's own aperture explains much of the remaining discrepancy. The calibrated light comparison also extracts the rotation-odd part. This addresses several confounding effects but still calls for a prospective, matched-aperture comparison with an independently calibrated clock. The smooth-gradient null did pass in the tested configurations. [GC57 scorecard](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/results/EX-GC57/summary.json)

A resting spin packet under links alone has a splitting equivalent to only 5–11% of the local rotation rate, decreasing with increasing packet width. The small signal follows the orbital angular momentum contained in the localized spinor. This gives GC58 a well-defined target. The reported frequencies are inferred from energy splittings and, for orbital matter, a torque expectation. Direct time traces of a freely precessing spin or orbital plane would test the interpretation independently. [GC57 probe implementation](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/src/exgc57.py)

## GC58 — One local interaction couples spin in both directions

The added plaquette term rotates a spinor in proportion to the discrete curl of the shift. The same term differentiated with respect to the shift adds a spin circulation to the source on links. For the two packet widths, the inferred spin rotation per unit total angular momentum reaches 0.9997–1.0001 of the local rate. The stationary spin source gives total angular momentum 0.49877, while the spin-balanced orbital response changes by at most 0.011%. The co-moving force ratio remains 0.9967 and the energy-drift check passes. All six registered predictions and four checks pass. The agreement between the source and the probe coupling is particularly important after GC54’s reciprocity problem. [GC58 plan](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/spec/EX-GC58_plan.md) · [GC58 scorecard](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/results/EX-GC58/summary.json)

Three limits matter for interpretation:

- **The coefficient ¼ was supplied.** Reproducing total angular momentum tests the implementation of the chosen coupling. Stronger cross-checks are that the conjugate source carries the same spin contribution and that the previously tested orbital and moving-pair observables survive. This describes spin coupling to frame rotation. An independent torsion degree of freedom has yet to be introduced.
- **Event ordering agrees to first order.** The quadratic single-step order spread is expected when local unitary updates have the same summed generator but do not commute. It supports convergence of the split evolution. Exact order independence at finite tick size or causal invariance of an asynchronous network requires a separate test. The field offsets supplied to these events still come from a prescribed field law.
- **The spin-source far-field readout is a field sum.** The code computes twice the sum of link offsets around the loop and compares it with the dipole of the measured source angular momentum. The static solver also receives that angular momentum for its boundary treatment. The reported 0.1% agreement is a field consistency test. An operational clock arrival or the motion of a second spin in an independently converged field would provide a stronger source-to-detector test. ## Next experiment

**Run a spin-source and spin-probe experiment from end to end.** Prepare a stationary polarized source and a separate transverse spin probe. Predict the precession rate from their separation, the measured source profile and the already fixed coupling. Evolve the probe and extract the angle from its transverse spin components over time. Reverse the source polarization, include an unpolarized source, and compare with links alone. Vary the box and boundary treatment, check the gradient null, and use an independently calibrated light loop sampling the same region. This would combine the source, propagation and detector tests presently performed in separate calculations. A later event-level transport law for the shift could then be scored against the same observable without refitting.

I reviewed both plans, implementations, results notes and saved scorecards on the experiment branch. I did not rerun the simulations.
