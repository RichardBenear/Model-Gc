# Independent review of EX-GC60

**GC60 gives Model Gc a predictive calibration for its spin probe.** At a new diagonal position in the GC59 source field, adding the independently calibrated shear response explains a sizable discrepancy in the spin rate. This is a reason to retain the current coupling as a benchmark. The stronger claims that the detector is fully characterized and that its entire off-axis discrepancy is a coordinate convention go beyond the experiment. [Registered plan](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/spec/EX-GC60_plan.md) · [Experiment report](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/results/EX-GC60/EX-GC60_note.md)

## The strongest result is the prediction at a new position

I reconstructed the diagonal prediction from the saved width-3 rotation and shear coefficients and the two recorded gradients. Rates below are in inverse simulation time. The last column uses the measured rate as the denominator. | Readout | Rotation contribution | Shear contribution | Measured | Error using rotation alone |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Spin angle | 5.96466 × 10⁻⁶ | 1.83464 × 10⁻⁶ | 7.80098 × 10⁻⁶ | −23.54% |
| Total angular momentum angle | 5.96446 × 10⁻⁶ | 0.03916 × 10⁻⁶ | 5.96749 × 10⁻⁶ | −0.051% |

For spin, the two-term prediction misses by only 0.022%. The diagonal geometry therefore discriminates between the two predictions for this readout. For total angular momentum, both predictions pass the 3% gate, and rotation alone is closer. The total-angular-momentum result supports its use as a rotation detector, while providing little leverage on its small shear coefficient. Both comparisons use gradients of the solved lattice field, so their precision measures how well the detector responds to a given field. It does not determine the accuracy of the gravitational field law.

## What GC60 resolves from GC59

- In prescribed rigid rotation, the full coupling gives spin and total-angular-momentum rates within 1% of the target over the three widths tested. In shear, the normalized spin response falls from 0.09274 to 0.04565 as the width increases from 3 to 5 sites. The total-angular-momentum angle is much less sensitive. The two tested orientations are related by a cubic-axis permutation. General tilt angles and other gradient components remain untested. [Scorecard](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/results/EX-GC60/summary.json)
- Halving the time step changes the GC59 equatorial spin rate by 0.0263%. That convergence check makes time stepping an implausible explanation for the roughly 22.5% shortfall against the GC59 free-space predictor. The equatorial calibration comparison is retrospective. The diagonal site provides the new test. [Time-step comparison](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/results/EX-GC60/EX-GC60_D.json)
- The recorded score is six of eight composite predictions and three of three checks. P2 misses the links-only versus full-coupling tolerance at the largest width. The recomputation locates a curl artifact at the box faces. A larger-box run at fixed width would directly test whether that artifact accounts for the discrepancy. For P5 the scorecard gives a maximum relative orientation difference of 2.45 × 10⁻⁸ across scored entries, comfortably inside the gate, though larger than the report's blanket 10⁻¹⁰ figure. ## The gauge test leaves one important question open

P6b makes a genuinely discriminating prediction. Replacing the momentum coupling with the first-order time-resynchronization coupling was expected to leave less than 10% of the shear signal. It leaves **93.13%**. This rules out the proposed coupling difference as the dominant origin of the raw spin drift in this configuration. The remaining 6.87% is measured at one width and orientation. Attributing its size to the difference in lattice currents requires an independent prediction of that residual. [Gauge-run data](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/results/EX-GC60/EX-GC60_B.json)

P6a compares evolution under the commutator Hamiltonian with free evolution conjugated by the same transformation. At readout the driver applies the transformation to the state and calls the ordinary spin-angle function. This is a sound internal check of the first-order evolution. It does not independently compare the same physical detector, with its spin axes and synchronization transformed, in the two descriptions. Also, the registered criterion names the angle history, whereas the scoring variable uses only the final angle difference. The fitted rates agree closely, but intermediate discrepancies can cancel in a slope and at an endpoint. The saved gauge output omits the histories needed to check their maximum discrepancy. I would record P6a as **endpoint and slope agreement, full-history gate unverified**. [Gauge implementation](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/src/exgc60.py) · [Saved comparison](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/results/EX-GC60/EX-GC60_B.json)

## Recommended next steps

1. **Close the detector question with a matched physical readout.** Start from one free preparation and transform both the state and the two spin-component operators into the resynchronized description. Compare their component histories and inferred angle at corresponding readouts. Run pure shear and genuine rotation, save every sample, and score the maximum discrepancy over time. Verify that the transformed detector removes the pure-coordinate signal while retaining sensitivity to genuine rotation. Repeat at a second shear strength to measure the order of the residual.
2. **Use total angular momentum as the primary benchmark for a propagating shift.** Keep the existing coupling and static solution as references. Switch on a localized source current, measure arrival at several distances, then compare the settled field and the axial and diagonal detector rates with the static predictions without refitting. Vary boundary distance to separate propagation from reflection. A local propagation law would address a missing step toward emergent gravity. GC60 itself uses a prescribed or statically solved field.
3. **Bound the calibration's range.** Repeat the width-5 shear comparison in a larger box, then use a tilted spin and an off-symmetry position to test a predeclared vector prediction. Retain raw spin as a secondary readout. These tests would show where the two-coefficient calibration transfers and where additional detector response is needed.

Review scope included the plan, implementation, scoring and recomputation scripts, report and saved numerical outputs at experiment commit ae58566. I independently reconstructed the diagonal prediction and rotation-only comparison from saved data. I did not rerun the field or matter simulations.
