# Independent review of EX-GC47

**GC47 meets the immediate goal of observing attraction in the coupled model.** The microscopic source profiles of two bound pairs produce an attractive interaction through the prescribed GC44 fields. A released probe accelerates inward, and two released pairs reduce their separation. The field constraint, coupling strength and vacuum prescription are inputs. The result establishes that the coupling moves the matter used in this programme. [Plan](../spec/EX-GC47_plan.md) · [Results](../results/EX-GC47/EX-GC47_note.md)

## Static interaction

At L = 32 the extended-pair cross energy agrees with the point-charge prediction using the same periodic Green function to within 0.14% over the scored far-field range. The spatial charge adds about 1.68% to the identical-pair dust coefficient. The zero-coupling control and independent small-box recomputation support the implementation. Reciprocity and linearity also follow from the symmetric linear kernel. The absolute periodic potential at fixed separation still shifts substantially with box size. A direct force-versus-distance and box-size sweep would give a stronger test of the inverse-square regime than the potential ratio alone. [GC47 results](../results/EX-GC47/EX-GC47_note.md) · [Implementation](../src/exgc47.py)

## Motion and inertia

For a fixed source, the measured accelerations agree with the force times the fitted pair-band curvature to 0.8–2.6%. When both pairs are released, their separation decreases by about 0.045 lattice sites. The experiment evolves a relative-coordinate Hamiltonian with a precomputed interaction. Opposite momenta are built into the zero-total-momentum representation. The small-box microscopic check validates low-lying energy shifts to 1.9% within the projected carrier model. Direct microscopic release dynamics and breakup probabilities remain unmeasured. [Release output](../results/EX-GC47/C.json) · [Implementation](../src/exgc47.py)

The most consequential result is the scale of the acceleration. In the adopted lattice units the dispersion gives inertial-to-rest-energy ratios of 8,496 and 7,778. Both probes therefore accelerate at only about 0.00012 times the reference lapse-gradient acceleration. A common choice of speed units affects an absolute mass-to-energy comparison, so a relativistic interpretation needs the independently calibrated signal speed in those same units. The relative B1/B2 acceleration is already a same-unit comparison. It differs from one by 5.5–8.5%, missing the registered prediction of a difference greater than 20%. The central force measurement is more secure than the exact size of this composition dependence. [Results](../results/EX-GC47/EX-GC47_note.md)

## Two issues to resolve

1. **The launch packet crosses a periodic seam with appreciable weight.** The code wraps the coordinate about the origin, then subtracts the launch center when constructing the Gaussian. For L = 24, launch center 8 and width 3, the probability density at x = 11 is about 61% of its peak. The adjacent periodic site x = −12 is assigned a displacement of −20 instead of +4. This introduces an artificial discontinuity and high momenta. Subtracting a field-off trajectory helps with common drift, but cannot guarantee cancellation of its influence on the field response. Periodize the packet about its own center, track a periodic or unwrapped centroid, and repeat at larger L, several widths and shorter fitting windows. Time-step halving tests another error entirely. [Release implementation](../src/exgc47.py)
2. **The strain form factor misses its normalization gate by 0.8%.** Within a parameter-dependent projected Hamiltonian, the strain derivative also acts on the projected interaction. Hellmann–Feynman still applies to the derivative of the full projected operator. Compute the local form factor from that derivative, or check it in the expanded upper-band basis. The estimated effect on the present cross energy is small, about 0.014%, but consistency matters when resolving composition-dependent corrections. [Results](../results/EX-GC47/EX-GC47_note.md)

## Recommended next experiment

First repeat the corrected release with the same matter and source, reporting energy drift and force convergence. Then test a massive lattice band whose low-momentum dispersion has a speed fixed independently by the signal sector. The central prediction in common lattice units is **2 t_pair E_rest = c_signal²**. Measure the curvature, rest energy, rest-frame strain charge and release acceleration independently, first for a free carrier, then for a bound pair of different composition. This tests whether the same microscopic parameters give both a near-dust source and the required inertia. Refitting G separately for each body would obscure that test.
