# Independent review of EX-GC53

**GC53 demonstrates a rotation-sensitive link shift and passes a quantitative moving-pair benchmark that the scalar model missed.** Its strongest result is the change in the transverse force on co-moving matter. The rotating-ring experiment establishes the sign of a frame-dragging signal, while its far-field magnitude and angular pattern still need a converged measurement. The shift equation and its normalization are inputs. [Plan and amendment](../spec/EX-GC53_plan.md) · [Results](../results/EX-GC53/EX-GC53_note.md)

## Moving pairs

At V = 0.2 the scalar model's normalized transverse force was 1.1152 against a target of one. With the shift strength set to four, GC53 gives 0.9975. All three tested speeds pass the 2% gate, and switching the shift off recovers the GC52 trace. I independently refitted the saved pair-minus-single momentum traces using the same quadratic estimator on successively longer intervals starting at zero. For windows ending at 4, 8, 12 and 16, the normalized ratio stays in 0.9975–0.9988 at V = 0.2 and 0.9894–0.9921 at V = 0.3. The improvement survives this choice of fitting window. The data establish consistency for one transverse-force observable with motion along a lattice axis. General absence of a preferred frame would require independent orientations, longitudinal dynamics and additional source configurations. [Momentum histories](../results/EX-GC53/E.json) · [Driver](../src/exgc53.py)

The inferred strength 3.91 interpolates between the same two endpoint results, at strengths zero and four. It quantifies how close the chosen normalization comes to correcting the force. It provides no independent microscopic determination of that normalization. A third simulated strength would test the assumed linearity.

## Ring and clock

Reversing the orbital current reverses the loop signal with the same scalar source, and the spin-balanced nonrotating control gives a negligible result. The matter–shift coupling passes its derivative check. Those controls show that the rotating source supplies the handedness. The 0.03% tilt result compares normalized loop sums against a dipole evaluated on each corresponding staircase path. It has a narrower scope than absolute orientation independence of clock arrivals. The amended small-loop scoring uses field sums, and enclosing loops additionally have propagated pulse timings. [Scorecard](../results/EX-GC53/summary.json) · [Amendment](../spec/EX-GC53_plan.md)

The registered dipole slopes, axis-to-plane ratio and amplitude fail. Finite source size explains much of the difference. For example, at in-plane distance 24 the free-space field of the actual current is 25.3% above the dipole estimate, while the lattice result is another 4.8% above that free-space field. At the nearest in-plane loop the latter residual is 14.4%. This decomposition supports the finite-ring explanation but leaves boundary and discretization errors to be separated. The boosted-source check C0 also misses its pointwise tolerance. Agreement between two linear solvers checks implementation of the same boundary problem. [Saved comparisons](../results/EX-GC53/summary.json)

The uniform-clock calibration passes, but the registered weak-lapse, pure-gauge and single-side controls fail. The conserved-energy centroid and lattice dispersion correction improve the weak-lapse interpretation. The pure-gauge residual is especially important: a pattern representing only a change of clock synchronization should contribute zero to a closed timing loop. Test a smooth discrete gradient, compare its algebraic loop sum with actual counterpropagating pulses, and vary spacing, wavelength and amplitude. A failure that persists in the continuum limit would change the interpretation of the clock observable. [GC53 results](../results/EX-GC53/EX-GC53_note.md)

## What to do next

1. **Finish the rotating-source benchmark.** Vary ring radius and box size independently at fixed measured angular momentum, resolve the current profile, and use the actual-current free-space reference or converged multipole boundary data. Register both finite-source and dipole comparisons. Read actual pulse arrivals as well as field sums and carry the gauge control through the same analysis.
2. **Use an independent matter probe.** Predict the precession of an orbital plane or a calibrated gyroscope outside the ring, with the strength already fixed. Reverse the source spin and repeat at several distances and orientations. Agreement would connect the clock circulation to local inertial-frame dragging.
3. **Keep the microscopic mechanism a separate prediction.** An ordered-firing rule should give zero directed offset in the stationary spin-balanced vacuum and an offset odd in the measured source current. Fix its normalization from the event statistics before scoring it against the ring and moving-pair benchmarks. GC53 measures the vector shift. Independent torsion would require a closure-defect observable or an explicit spin–torsion coupling. Tensor radiation would require dynamical spatial shear and polarization measurements.
