# Independent review of EX-GC59

**GC59 demonstrates spin-sourced precession inside the prescribed shift-field model.** A polarized source packet supplies a microscopic current, that current produces a static shift, and a separately evolved probe develops a measurable spin angle. Reversing the source reverses the precession. This completes the source-to-detector test proposed after GC58. The source is held fixed and the field solved statically, so the run tests the matter coupling and detector response, leaving field propagation and backreaction open. ## What holds up

The on-axis probe reaches 98.7% and 97.7% of the free-space weak-field prediction at distances 12 and 16. It agrees more closely with the curl of the actual lattice field averaged over the probe. The main runs use zero boundary values, a stronger source test than supplying the source dipole at the boundary. At distance 16, enlarging the zero-boundary box improves the free-space ratio to 0.991. The dipole-boundary result is 1.001 but differs from the main run by 2.48%, missing P4. These comparisons support a boundary contribution to the on-axis deficit. Two sizes give limited leverage on its extrapolation. [Scorecard](../results/EX-GC59/summary.json)

I independently refitted the saved source-odd spin angle, half the difference between the up and down traces, over the first and last halves of the run. The rates relative to the free-space prediction were 0.987 and 0.984 on axis at 12, 0.976 and 0.978 on axis at 16, and **0.746 and 0.764 in the plane** at 16. The axial result is stable under this check. The in-plane shortfall survives both fits, and its precise size depends on the time window. Links alone give about 1% of the full source-to-probe signal when the plaquette coupling is removed at both ends. The unpolarized null uses the average of the up and down fields, appropriate for a linear mixture and partly guaranteed by that construction. ## What the plane and gradient failures mean

The registered in-plane rate is **0.775 of prediction**, and the pure-gradient run gives **7.7% of the source signal** against a 1% limit. A post hoc calibration in rigid rotation and curl-free shear predicts the in-plane spin rate accurately. This identifies a sensitivity of the implemented finite packet and spin readout. It does not uniquely establish its microscopic cause. In particular, the assertion that changing the shift generator will cure it has yet to be tested. Total angular momentum is much less sensitive to the shear calibration, although its in-plane result still differs from the free-space reference by about 5.5%. The gradient normalization was taken over a region extending toward the source, rather than matched at the detector as planned. The 7.7% is therefore an operational null failure for that setup, not a universal coupling coefficient. [Results and post hoc diagnostics](../results/EX-GC59/EX-GC59_note.md)

Four of seven predictions pass. The calibrated light delay has the right sign and a ratio of 0.988 to the corrected free-space loop predictor. Its normalization uses a separate uniform-shift calibration, and a singular self-term was removed from the comparator before scoring. Random event orders reproduce the on-axis Hamiltonian rate for the two seeds tested. The norm check fails at about 1.07 × 10⁻⁷ in both integrators, which share an RK4 matter step. The smaller-step axial recomputation supports the rate there. An equivalent in-plane convergence check would strengthen the shear diagnosis. ## Recommended next test

Keep the Hamiltonian fixed for one decisive detector test. Prescribe three local fields with independently controlled gradients: rigid rotation, symmetric shear and their sum. Match those gradients over the probe density, then vary packet width and orientation at fixed local field. Track spin, orbital angular momentum and their sum in the same run. For the synchronization test, transform the state preparation, detector axes and readout time along with the field, and compare with a genuine rotation as a positive control. Freeze the calibrated response and predict the spin-angle history at a new off-axis position before running it. This distinguishes finite-packet angular-momentum transfer and detector conventions from an error in the generator. Preserve the zero-boundary on-axis experiment as the benchmark for a future microscopic shift-transport law.
