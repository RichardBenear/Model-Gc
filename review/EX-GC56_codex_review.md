# Independent review of EX-GC56

**GC56 establishes how a prescribed link shift transports the tested Dirac packets. It also extends the co-moving force benchmark. The light result misses one registered tolerance, and an independent exact-time check localizes that discrepancy to the packet experiment and its comparator.** [Plan](../spec/EX-GC56_plan.md) · [Results](../results/EX-GC56/EX-GC56_note.md)

## Matter transport and moving pairs

Coupling the shift to nearest-neighbor lattice momentum gives a centered spatial difference acting identically on every spinor component. In uniform shift the energy change is proportional to −β sin k and the velocity change is −β cos k. The numerical drift follows the packet average of that expression to 7.2 × 10⁻⁵ across the tested matter cases. This establishes independence of species at matched momentum distribution. The cosine factor also specifies where the continuum interpretation as a common drift breaks down: packets at different lattice momenta generally have different shifts. The exact operator identity verifies the selected discretization. It supplies no independent derivation of the shift amplitude from firing events.

The energy-current comparison is particularly informative. Its drift follows the curvature of the squared band energy. For Wilson matter at the same central momentum, varying the mass changes the drift from 1.32 to 2.22 times the momentum prediction. Under the requirement that a uniform shift carry all these matter packets alike, momentum is the better **probe** coupling. Selecting the **source** and its normalization still requires a reciprocity argument or an event model with a conserved joint current. [Scorecard](../results/EX-GC56/summary.json) · [Implementation](../src/exgc56.py)

The normalized co-moving transverse forces are 1.0031 at half well depth, 0.9998 at the held-out speed and 1.0039 at the narrower packet width. All pass their registered gates. Relative to baseline, the half-depth change of 0.00451 is close to its 0.005 gate. The three variations extend one force observable with fixed coupling. They do not test arbitrary boosts, orientations, nonuniform shifts, gyroscope precession or the field’s propagation law. The zero-shift C1 check runs the same Hamiltonian twice, making it principally a determinism check. [Saved scores](../results/EX-GC56/summary.json)

## Independent check of the light residual

For a uniform shift the wave equations in the driver diagonalize in Fourier space. Each mode evolves as the unshifted lattice oscillator of frequency 2|sin(k/2)| multiplied by exp(i β t sin k). I used this exact time evolution with the driver's launch, energy-density centroid, width 16, box length 512 and observation time 100. The resulting measured-to-predicted drift ratios are **0.99423945** and **0.99720657** at central momenta 0.25 and 0.5, reproducing the recorded RK4 results to better than 10⁻⁷. The first value still fails the registered 0.5% tolerance. Time integration is effectively excluded as its explanation. [Light initialization and readout](../src/exgc56.py)

Two distinct choices enter the comparison. The driver holds the initial coordinate-time derivative fixed, so the canonical wave variable w changes with β. It then measures a β-dependent energy centroid against a prediction weighted by the squared Fourier amplitude of the displacement envelope. In a diagnostic variation I held the initial canonical pair (u,w) fixed while retaining the same energy readout and predictor. The ratios shifted to **1.00629** and **1.00023**. Launch convention matters at the scale of the discrepancy. This variation alone does not cure the low-momentum miss. The appropriate next check is an exact one-way lattice eigenpacket with spectral weights derived for the observable actually measured. Compare its phase/group delay and its energy centroid separately, and repeat over packet width. This can distinguish a readout effect from a departure in transport without adjusting β.

## Implications for frame dragging

A uniform imposed shift tests how a packet responds locally. Rotation requires a circulating, spatially varying shift generated by matter. After settling the light readout, freeze the momentum coupling and compare a rotating source’s predicted closed-loop light delay with an independent matter-precession measurement. Reverse the current and run a smooth pure-gradient control with a consistently synchronized detector. A microscopic event-rule claim would additionally require deriving the signed link transfer, its source strength and its evolution from the same counting prescription before scoring that benchmark.

I reviewed the plan, source, results and scorecard and independently propagated the two uniform-shift light cases in Fourier space. I did not rerun the full matter or co-moving simulations.
