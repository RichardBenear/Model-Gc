# Independent review of EX-GC48, EX-GC49 and EX-GC50

GC48 improves the relation between energy and inertia for a free particle. GC49 shows how the choice of what neighbors transmit controls nonlinear gravity. GC50 identifies which part of the vacuum response follows from the clock rule. The three experiments use different matter and field assumptions. A single model that binds matter, carries its field, and predicts its motion remains to be demonstrated.

## GC48 — Correct the interpretation of composite inertia

The free Wilson–Dirac band gives the registered curvature ratio R = 1 + rm. With the massless speed calibrated from packets, the continuum relation is recovered as rm becomes small. Scaling the whole Wilson derivative with spatial strain also gives a rest-packet stress that tends toward zero as the packet widens. The m = 0.2 rest-packet gate failed at the registered width and improved on the post hoc wider packets. Applying the older seven-band network’s maximum group speed to the GC47 bodies still yields R ≈ 0.004. That is a meaningful within-network speed reference, though identifying it with a physical light signal requires a propagation measurement. [Plan](../spec/EX-GC48_plan.md) · [Results](../results/EX-GC48/EX-GC48_note.md)

**The results note inverts the composite mass ratio.** The code defines R_c = 2 t_c E₀/c² and the dispersion defines inertial mass m_i = 1/(2t_c). Hence R_c = (E₀/c²)/m_i. The saved range 0.276–0.787 means the composite inertia is **1.27–3.62 times E₀/c²**, rather than 28–79% of it. For example, the m = 0.5, weak-binding row has E₀ = 0.980 and t_c = 0.40155, giving m_i ≈ 1.245 in units with c = 1. The dispersion calculation stands. A quantitative free-fall prediction additionally requires the probe’s spatial coupling and the source fields, especially since Q_a/E₀ reaches 2.24–9.39 here. [Saved composite outputs](../results/EX-GC48/C.json) · [Implementation](../src/exgc48.py)

The large kinetic and negative interaction energies nearly cancel even in the weakly bound states. This makes the chosen contact composite sensitive to lattice-scale physics. The weakest light-mass point also shows significant size dependence. The registered potential-energy estimate passes three of four cases and the spectator curvature estimate passes two of four. These results motivate changing the interaction and checking convergence under lattice refinement. They do not isolate instantaneous binding as the unique cause.

## GC49 — The nonlinear coefficient distinguishes rules, but cannot select one uniquely

Transporting the firing rate gives continuum β = −0.5. Transporting the normalized optical slowness gives β = 1, the GR value. Only the slowness case passed the registered lattice β fit. Removing a source-linear lattice contribution after scoring brings all four numerical estimates close to their continuum values. The post hoc explanation can be tested prospectively with new source strengths, orientations and lattice spacings. The leading source coefficient provides a flux/solver check in a problem with a supplied source and Gauss-law boundary values. [Plan](../spec/EX-GC49_plan.md) · [Results](../results/EX-GC49/EX-GC49_note.md)

There is an identifiability limit. In the continuum exterior, the harmonic variable satisfies F′(N) = N² f′(N). With f′(1) normalized to one, expansion about N = 1 gives β = −[1 + f″(1)]/2. Thus β = 1 fixes f″(1) = −3. Infinitely many transport laws share that derivative. A cubic addition in N − 1 leaves β unchanged while changing the strong-field prediction. Slowness is selected among the four registered candidates. A microscopic derivation or an independent higher-order observable is needed to distinguish it more generally.

The delay comparison tests a one-dimensional path in the **f = N** background. It gives 0.05% agreement at the highest tested wavenumber, while the registered slope fails. An optical and gravitational signal propagating through the same **slowness-rule** background in three dimensions would connect the static and dynamical claims. The present study has no matter-derived source or propagating interaction that binds the GC48 composite. [Delay outputs](../results/EX-GC49/B.json) · [Implementation](../src/exgc49.py)

## GC50 — A uniform clock rescaling removes one vacuum response

At fixed spatial geometry and positive uniform N, H(N) = N H(1) implies E_vac(N) = N E_vac(1). Its second lapse derivative is exactly zero, while its first derivative generally remains nonzero. The identity explains the missing clock-sector mass term and leaves the background vacuum energy and mixed and spatial responses to be handled. The pair network retains a small nonzero q² clock stiffness. The Dirac sea missed the registered finite-grid intercept tolerance, with the larger post hoc grid supporting the predicted suppression. A conclusion about the effective gravitational coupling requires the full coupled clock–space kernel and a stated subtraction prescription. Replacing a single clock stiffness in GC47 gives a conditional sensitivity estimate. [Plan](../spec/EX-GC50_plan.md) · [Results](../results/EX-GC50/EX-GC50_note.md) · [Kernel checks](../results/EX-GC50/checks.json)

Pairing the Dirac spectrum with oscillator frequencies built from the same operator gives exact cancellation on the tested backgrounds. This demonstrates a sufficient construction. Protection of the pairing by a microscopic symmetry, particularly after interactions are added, requires its own demonstration. Also, the identity linking the older coincidence weight to the clock–space split needs a uniform-field qualification: for unequal endpoint lapses, the squared arithmetic mean differs from their product by (N_v − N_w)²/4. This vanishes for the uniform susceptibility but matters for gradient terms.

## Priority recommendations

1. Correct the GC48 inertia interpretation. Use both m_i c²/E₀ and its explicitly labeled reciprocal in subsequent tables. Compare free and bound packets in the same independently calibrated weak field, including spatial charge.
2. For the next binding test, choose a finite interaction range resolved by several lattice sites. Match the free dispersion, binding energy and physical range while refining the lattice. Measure bound-state curvature, radius and total strain derivative. A propagating mediator can then be tested against a matched static interaction, including its field energy and stress.
3. Before combining GC49 and GC50, derive the transported quantity, source coupling and vacuum response for one declared model. Then test propagation of a localized source change, the static exterior coefficient, and the response of a distant probe with the same parameters.

Review scope includes the plans, implementations, results notes and selected saved outputs. The mass conversion and transport-law expansion are independent checks. The simulations were not rerun.
