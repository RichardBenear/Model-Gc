# Independent review of EX-GC41A/B and EX-GC40C

2026-11-26 | Codex

**GC41A validates the deterministic time delay more convincingly than GC40B did. GC41B shows that the median of shared-clock first arrivals cannot yet stand in for that deterministic model. GC40C gives strong evidence that the tested hub is a short-range defect.** Keep the deterministic delay as a benchmark, resolve the statistics of GC41B, and move the bending work toward a signal with a defined amplitude or phase.

## GC41A — Time delay passes. Bending still fails.

The strongest result is the **absolute delay**. At the two scored impact parameters, all eight mean delays agree with the matched continuum calculation to within 2.3%, across both propagation rules and both lens strengths. This is stronger than GC40B’s factor-of-two ratio because each rule has to reproduce its own delay. The continuum calculation uses the same prescribed lapse and rate law, so the experiment tests whether the random graph reproduces that optical model. The gravitational profile and spatial embedding still enter as inputs. The 3% pass is based on point estimates. Several individual confidence intervals extend beyond that tolerance. [GC41 plan](../spec/EX-GC41_plan.md) · [Scored data](../results/EX-GC41/scored.json)

The bending failure deserves more than an explanation based on noise. At b = 40, **all four** mean slopes are high, and three individual bootstrap intervals exclude the continuum value. They use the same graphs and receiver windows, so these are correlated discrepancies. That pattern warrants a check for a shared aperture or estimator effect. The code fits a slope across a receiver strip four units thick, while the continuum reference samples a line at its center. The result is a finite-window arrival-time slope, a proxy for bending. Compare both models using the same receiver coordinates and regression, then test the residual at shifted receiver planes and mirrored impact parameters on fresh graphs. The isotropy result also remains a precision miss under the registered gate. [GC41A source](../src/exgc41a.py) · [Scored data](../results/EX-GC41/scored.json)

## GC41B — Shared clocks change transport. The delay excess needs replication.

The shared-clock implementation addresses the main GC40B concern. Each site now has one event stream used by all its incident links, and the flat and lens runs share underlying random draws. The clearest result is in the **one-site flat control**. Shared-clock propagation gives a speed of **2.50**, against **3.63** with independent edge draws. The sender’s next firing makes all its outgoing links eligible at the same time, whereas independent draws let the earliest route benefit from several separate chances. The algorithm check compares two solvers that share the same crossing routine. It verifies the arrival-time search against a second solver, and a direct chronological event simulation would independently check the crossing semantics. [GC41B source](../src/exgc41b.py) · [Recorded checks](../results/EX-GC41/41b_checks.json)

The two-site median-front delay is **15.6% high at both impact parameters**, so it fails the registered 5% point-estimate gate. My exploratory paired bootstrap of the ten saved graphs gives approximate 95% intervals for median-front delay divided by deterministic delay of **1.01–1.31** at b = 10 and **0.99–1.31** at b = 20. Those intervals are wide, and the two readings share graphs and histories. I would report a **suggestive excess**, with the size and generality still open. The reported ratios **2.23 and 2.45** average ratios graph by graph. Dividing the ensemble-mean delays instead gives **2.14 and 2.34**. Both are above the deterministic values of **1.89 and 2.01**, but the choice matters when the one-site denominator fluctuates. The next plan should specify the primary ratio and report paired intervals for the absolute delays first. [Saved GC41B runs](../results/EX-GC41/41b_run.json) · [GC41 results note](../results/EX-GC41/EX-GC41_note.md)

## GC40C — A convincing local-defect result

The pure-pair control behaves as expected: the hub affects its own modes and their link partners. In both tested dressed, gapped backgrounds, the link-energy response falls sharply with distance, the detectable two-hub interaction is short range, and increasing the box from 16 to 32 sites leaves the resolved profile essentially unchanged. The fitted exponential profile beats 1/r by large margins. The registered correlation-length clause still fails at the stronger dressing, so I would treat the proposed half-correlation-length explanation as a hypothesis. The qualitative short-range finding is much stronger than that fitted coefficient. [GC40C plan](../spec/EX-GC40C_plan.md) · [GC40C results](../results/EX-GC40C/EX-GC40C_note.md)

The hub is imposed by changing a local coupling. Its negative ground-state energy shift compares two Hamiltonians, and the in-gap levels establish trapping by the defect. A particle interpretation would require evidence for a mobile excitation in a fixed Hamiltonian. The run also omitted the planned test of whether the response amplitude tracks energy or coupling strength, and the gapless case was unresolved because of Fermi-level degeneracy. The conclusion I would carry forward is that **these static hubs in the tested gapped Gaussian networks produce no detectable Newtonian far field**. The gapless and collective possibilities remain open. [GC40C source](../src/exgc40c.py) · [GC40C results](../results/EX-GC40C/EX-GC40C_note.md)

## What I would run next

1. **Reanalyze the saved optics results before scaling up.** For GC41A, test matched receiver sampling and mirrored apertures, then freeze the estimator before scoring fresh graphs. For GC41B, choose the ratio of ensemble-mean delays as the primary ratio, use paired graph-level intervals, and check convergence separately in the number of histories and the number of graphs. This will show whether the 16% excess survives a more stable measurement.
2. **Test a finite-width pulse with a defined amplitude or phase.** Predefine a pulse arrival and an outgoing direction, then calibrate the propagation speed and dispersion in flat space. On the same graphs, compare delay and bending for two lens strengths without refitting the flat calibration. This tests a physically distinct signal from taking more medians of earliest arrivals.
3. **Close the gapped hub branch and make the next gravity test a source-law test.** If a lapse equation is proposed, write its source and conservation rule before running it. Compare two differently constructed localized sources with the same integrated excess energy. Require the same far-field coefficient and, in the linear regime, superposition for two separated sources. If a Poisson equation is supplied as part of the model, obtaining 1/r mainly checks its implementation. The meaningful test is whether the microscopic energy couples to that field with one coefficient across sources.

Review scope includes the plans, source, results notes, and saved GC41 outputs. The bootstrap is a reanalysis of the saved data. The simulations were not rerun for this review.
