# How lattice packets probe relativistic gravity

**Yes. Published studies have evolved localized packets on lattices and measured effects predicted by general relativity (GR).** The nearest match depends on which part of Model Gc one wants to compare. Arrighi and Facchini give a three-dimensional lattice construction for a massive Dirac particle in a chosen spacetime. Strange examines what happens to the spin and orbital motion of a localized Dirac packet in a rotating frame. Herdeiro and colleagues compute the gravitational field of spinning Dirac matter. Across the primary papers examined, I found no single study combining the specific GC59–60 sequence of a polarized source on a cubic lattice, a static field computed from its discrete current, and a second finite packet whose precession is tested against rotation and shear calibrations. This was a targeted literature scan, so it establishes close precedents and leaves priority open. [Arrighi and Facchini (2017)](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic17/qic-17-910/0810-0824.pdf) · [Strange (2017)](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/64298/2/PhysRevA.96.052103.pdf) · [Herdeiro et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physletb.2019.134845)

## Three-dimensional lattice rules can reproduce curved-space propagation

**Arrighi and Facchini are the closest geometric comparison.** They construct local updates along three spatial axes whose long-wavelength limit is the massive Dirac equation in a curved spacetime. The researchers select the metric and use it to determine the lattice updates. In a complementary numerical demonstration, Di Molfetta, Brachet and Debbasch use one spatial dimension to follow radial propagation inside and outside a Schwarzschild black hole. Their packet probability follows the expected light rays. These works address a question that matters for Model Gc: once a geometry is specified, does a discrete wave propagate as GR predicts? Neither paper tests how the packet’s own spin generates the field felt by another packet. [Three-dimensional construction](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic17/qic-17-910/0810-0824.pdf) · [Schwarzschild packet simulations (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.88.042301)

Arnault and Debbasch also test a Dirac lattice model against a prescribed gravitational wave. They find that fermion polarization can change at wavelengths of a few lattice spacings. Their wave shears the **spatial metric** in a two-dimensional transverse plane. GC60 instead varies the **shift**, the part of the model geometry associated with local frame motion, and distinguishes its shear from its rotation. The two studies share an interest in polarization and lattice resolution, but they apply different geometric perturbations. [Arnault and Debbasch (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.00722) · [GC60 plan](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/spec/EX-GC60_plan.md)

## The GC60 rotation control has a close packet precedent

**Paul Strange’s 2017 paper is the first one I would read alongside GC60.** Strange constructs localized Dirac wave packets in a uniformly rotating frame, then switches the spin and orbital rotation couplings off in turn to see what each contributes. The combined coupling has the familiar form −Ω·(L + S), with L the orbital angular momentum and S the intrinsic spin. The packet examples use a continuum plane, and the rotation belongs to the observer’s frame in flat spacetime. This gives GC60 an independent comparison for its rigid-rotation control. A separate lattice precedent is Yamamoto and Hirono’s rotating-frame Wilson-fermion calculation. Its observable is angular momentum in the quantum chromodynamics vacuum, illustrating how the same lattice ingredients can serve a different experiment. [Strange, *Physical Review A* (2017)](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/64298/2/PhysRevA.96.052103.pdf) · [Yamamoto and Hirono, *Physical Review Letters* (2013)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.6292)

For **actual gravitational frame dragging**, Obukhov, Silenko and Teryaev derive quantum and classical spin precession in the weak stationary metric of a rotating body. They show how the angular velocity assigned to the spin depends on the observer’s local reference axes. Adler, Chen and Varani derive the weak gravitational coupling of intrinsic spin and orbital angular momentum for a Dirac fermion and find a gravitational gyromagnetic ratio of one for both. These papers supply a continuum prediction for a lattice gyroscope. They also sharpen GC60’s open question: what would an observer carrying specified axes and clocks actually record when the packet is sheared or the clocks are resynchronized? [Obukhov et al. (2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4367) · [Adler et al. (2012)](https://slac.stanford.edu/pubs/slacpubs/15000/slac-pub-15003.pdf) · [GC60 independent review](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/47c4f43/review/EX-GC60_codex_review.md)

## A spinning wave field can source its own geometry

**Herdeiro, Perapechka, Radu and Shnir are the closest source-side comparison.** They solve jointly for a localized spinning Dirac field and its stationary gravitational geometry using the Einstein–Dirac equations. Axial symmetry reduces their numerical grid to radius and polar angle. Their observables include the mass and angular momentum of the equilibrium configuration. GC59 instead prepares a source packet, holds it fixed, solves a static shift field from its current, and evolves a separate probe. The published result establishes a precedent for self-gravitating spinning Dirac matter and offers an independent source model for comparison. [Herdeiro et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05386) · [GC59 source and probe code](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/55fef93/src/exgc59.py)

A particularly clear answer to the literal **three-dimensional grid plus localized matter plus gravity** question comes from Guzmán. He evolves a localized complex scalar field coupled to the Einstein equations on a Cartesian grid, following oscillations, collapse and gravitational signals. Here the grid approximates continuum equations and the matter has spin zero. Figure 8 is especially relevant to Model Gc: changing the directional resolution of the grid excites a measurable quadrupolar signal. That gives a concrete reason to rotate the entire source–probe setup relative to the grid when interpreting small direction-dependent effects. [Guzmán, *Physical Review D* (2004), especially Fig. 8](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_151572_1/component/file_151571/content)

## What this means for the white paper

The defensible contribution to describe is **the particular source-to-detector test**. GC59 computes a shift from a polarized packet’s lattice current and measures the turning of another packet. GC60 separates the effects of rotation and shear on that finite detector and uses the calibration to predict its response at a new diagonal position. The existing results establish how the detector responds to a field obtained with the chosen static law. Whether that law arises from local propagation and how the field reacts back on its source remain open questions about emergent gravity. [GC59 review](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/9c4a888/review/EX-GC59_codex_review.md) · [GC60 results](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/ae58566/results/EX-GC60/EX-GC60_note.md)

A particularly informative comparison would use the **same probe twice**. First supply the independently calculated weak field of a rotating source, with detector axes and observation times specified, and compare its precession with the continuum prediction at several angles and separations. Then substitute the field computed from the GC59 source packet, keeping the detector, source angular momentum and normalization fixed. Rotate the setup relative to the lattice and refine the spacing at fixed physical packet width and separation. This would identify whether any persistent discrepancy enters through the matter detector, the generated field, or lattice resolution. [Continuum gyroscope benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4367) · [Classical two-body spin-precession benchmark](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.12.329)
