# Independent review of EX-GC63

**GC63 resolves a specific problem from GC54.** Evaluating the moving packet’s energy current in local clock units brings the co-moving pair’s transverse-force ratio from about 1.019 to 1.00277 at speed 0.2, with the previous coupling normalization held fixed. I would retain this as the orbital-source benchmark. The experiment tests the choice of current inside a clock well. The origin of the overall factor four, the intrinsic-spin source, and an entanglement origin of either remain open. ## What the moving pair establishes

The code replaces the lapse weights inside the current operator by ones. The matter and field still evolve in the nonuniform lapse. This is a well-controlled intervention relative to GC54. The initial current strength rises from 3.368 to 3.852 in canonical-momentum units. That recovery is largely built into the flat operator and identical initial packets. The more informative test is whether the evolved force follows. Its registered prediction, based on the earlier strength-to-force relation, was 1.002 ± 0.008. The result is within 0.001 of the line evaluated at the measured strength. Agreement at this single nonzero speed supports the specific boost test. It leaves general Lorentz invariance untested. [Current substitution](../src/exgc63.py) · [Inherited force and field routines](../src/exgc54.py) · [Scorecard](../results/EX-GC63/summary.json)

I independently subtracted the single-packet transverse momentum from the first packet’s momentum in the pair, fitted a quadratic in time for each speed, and formed the registered ratio from the linear coefficients. I obtained **1.002765** over times 0–16, **1.003608** over 0–8, **1.004055** over 0–12, and **1.000433** over 4–16. All pass the original band. The last fit extrapolates its slope back to time zero. This sensitivity check supports the improvement over GC54 and limits how precisely the residual 0.28% can be interpreted. A half-step run matters because the current is held fixed during each four-stage matter/field update. These fits reuse the saved trajectories. Four of five predictions and two of three checks passed. P2 and P3 score the same force ratio; C3 evaluates the same current routine with identical inputs. The velocity-reversal check C2 missed its tolerance (1.49 × 10⁻¹¹ against 10⁻¹²). Its size is negligible on the force scale. Its cause is undetermined. The check measures total current, allowing local errors to cancel. A spatially reflected packet comparison with the appropriate spin transformation, and a compensated sum, would distinguish a preparation or local-symmetry discrepancy from accumulated roundoff. ## What the spin result adds

The resting packet has essentially zero net counted current and a finite signed current dipole. These can coexist because circulating currents cancel in the total. The spin-up z moments are 0.022832 for the local-tick energy current, 0.024568 for canonical link momentum alone, and 0.249385 for the GC59 link-plus-plaquette source. Spin reversal reverses the moment. The count supplies **9.16%** of the reference, above the registered 5% ceiling. It tracks the orbital contribution reasonably well (92.9% of its moment) and misses most of the full source. A single dipole integral cannot establish equality of two local current distributions. [Both polarization records](../results/EX-GC63/EX-GC63_B.json)

There is a useful numerical prediction already in these data. Keeping the old plaquette contribution and replacing only the canonical orbital current by the counted one gives a moment ratio of (0.022832 + 0.249385 − 0.024568)/0.249385 ≈ **0.9930**. This is a prediction for the integrated moment of that hybrid source. A finite-distance probe can distinguish sources having almost identical dipoles. Freeze this number and test the axis and diagonal probes without adjusting the plaquette coefficient.

## How far the GC62 counting argument travels

GC62’s boost argument gives a clear target. If a weak static metric with ruler-to-clock ratio γ transforms as a Lorentz tensor, boosting it mixes its time and space components and requires a shift coefficient of magnitude 2(1 + γ) in the conventions used. With γ = 1 this gives four. GC63’s inherited solver explicitly sets the multiplier to four and evaluates an energy-current operator. It never generates forward and backward coincidence events and estimates the multiplier from their counts. “No new fit” describes the run accurately. Its effective strength of 3.852 is the supplied four times the measured current-to-momentum ratio. Whether converting endpoint events to local ticks yields this particular flat anticommutator in an inhomogeneous lapse remains a microscopic hypothesis. Also, matter responds through the canonical link momentum coupling while the source now uses the energy current. Deriving both from a common interaction or checking the corresponding exchange balance would materially strengthen the case for reciprocity. [Counting argument](../docs/EX-GC62_lambda4_counting.md) · [Source and matter evolution](../src/exgc54.py)

The connection to Cao, Carroll and Michalakis is a research direction. They relate mutual information to spatial geometry and entanglement perturbations to a spatial curvature equation. GC63 uses a fixed 3D lattice and measures a force ratio and current dipole. It samples no entanglement cuts, modular energies, or changes of dimension. It therefore leaves open GC62’s competing endpoint and plane counts, which coincide in three dimensions. ## Priorities

1. **Test whether the improved current transfers.** Freeze the rule and predict the force at additional positive and negative boosts, a second packet width, and two well depths. Include a smaller time step and report both the integrated current and its spatial distribution. Varying the depth directly tests the reason given for GC54’s deficit.
2. **Run the hybrid spin-source test.** Compare counted orbital current alone, plaquette current alone, and their sum with the same GC59 detector. Score the source dipole and precession separately. This checks whether the orbital replacement can coexist with the successful spin benchmark while preserving its normalization.
3. **Use an independent observable to test the origin of four.** If the priority is the Hilbert-space proposal, predeclare a boosted-cut modular-energy measurement with opposite source velocities and an independently fixed normalization. Alternatively, distinguish endpoint versus plane counts in four spatial dimensions, where light/clock is predicted to be 2 versus 3/2. Build the clock and signal from the proposed substrate and measure their response to the same perturbation. Feeding the target dimension-dependent weights into the update would make the agreement an identity. In two spatial dimensions use ruler-normalized observables because the proposed Newtonian clock coupling vanishes.
