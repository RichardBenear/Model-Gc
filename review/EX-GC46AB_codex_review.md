# Independent review of EX-GC46A/B and proposal for a two-body gravity test

**GC46 produces a bound carrier pair with small spatial charge and finite mobility. The next experiment should use two such pairs and measure the interaction between them.** Start with a controlled static energy measurement, then release the probe in the field generated by the source. This uses the existing matter and static field models. The result would establish gravitational interaction in the coupled model with its prescribed field equations and vacuum subtraction. ## GC46A — The four-band check matters

The enlarged rank-one solver corrects the important GC45 projection error. At the middle 15% binding point, η = δQ_a/E_b changes from −0.104 to −0.038 at fixed U, or −0.033 after matching the binding fraction. Strongly hybridized cases also show 13–18% changes in binding energy at fixed U and miss the registered 5% gate. The large binding responses at the strongly and weakly hybridized ends survive with opposite signs. The dense and independent recomputations support the numerical solver within the four-upper-band, frozen-sea model. ## GC46B — An approximately dust-like composite is available

At g = 0.0125, total Q_a/E is 0.21–0.28%, and the pair has a nonzero dispersion curvature. Halving g from 0.025 reduces that curvature by about fourfold. The absolute binding contribution is small even when η is order one, because E_b/E also shrinks. The data are consistent with a finite η limit. Agreement of the last two points is a convergence check, and the asymptote still depends on the chosen path with U retuned at fixed E_b/W. Also, at g = 0.1 the sign differs between m₀ = 0.5 and 0.7 for both tested binding fractions. Thus hybridization can drive a sign change at fixed m₀, and the on-site scale can affect the sign at fixed g. The curvature comes from one momentum step and should be refined before it is used to predict acceleration. No free-fall result follows from η alone. ## Proposed GC47 — Two bound pairs, with the field switched on and off

1. **Start with m₀ = 0.5, g = 0.025 and E_b/W = 0.15.** The saved row has E = 0.99141, Q_a = 0.00822, E_b = 0.00112 and pair dispersion coefficient about 5.88 × 10⁻⁵. This gives subpercent spatial charge and about four times the curvature of the weakest point. Refine the low-momentum dispersion and measure the pair's size and gap to breakup. Construct localized center-of-mass wave packets from the bound branch, with separation large compared with packet widths and pair size. Use independent copies of the two-species matter sector so the source and probe have no direct contact or exchange term between them. Both copies couple to the same fields. [GC46B data](https://github.com/RichardBenear/Model-Gc/blob/claude/project-thread-yzw1l2/results/EX-GC46/B.json)
2. **Measure a static interaction energy before trying trajectories.** With the GC44 two-field prescription, build spatial energy and strain-charge profiles from the localized pair states and solve for Φ and Ψ using the existing vacuum prescription. Measure the shift of the probe Hamiltonian in these fields at several separations. Subtract a source-off run and swap source and probe. In the bare, weak-field, far-field limit, the predicted cross energy is V₁₂(r) = −G(E₁E₂ + E₁Q_a₂ + Q_a₁E₂)/r. Use the measured Green function if the vacuum changes the kernel. For identical benchmark pairs, the two spatial cross terms add about 1.66% to the bare dust coefficient. Count the interaction once if using a total energy with both fields solved. Score the sign and coefficient, the corresponding inverse-square force, linear response in G, and agreement between orientations and larger boxes. Compare to the finite-box Green function before fitting 1/r. Include G = 0 and a one-body control to check for artifacts from localization and self-fields.
3. **Release the probe in the source-generated field.** First keep the source fixed and subtract a matched source-off trajectory. Evolve a probe packet with the strain and lapse couplings already specified by the matter Hamiltonian. For a packet near the dispersion minimum with E(K) ≈ E(0) + t_pair K², the predicted initial acceleration along the separation is −2t_pair ∂V₁₂/∂r. Compare the measured inward drift with that prediction, while checking that the potential variation across the pair is small compared with its breakup gap. Then release both bodies and update the static constraints from both densities, using reciprocal forces and convergence in time step, box size and packet width as checks. Keep the direct matter interaction disabled between sectors throughout.

The first two stages would show whether the existing microscopic matter states produce a measurable, reciprocal attraction through the prescribed fields. The release test would establish that the same coupling actually moves a bound body. Deriving the field equations and vacuum prescription from the network, and testing universality of free fall, are separate milestones. The finite-range binding experiment can follow after the first attraction benchmark.
