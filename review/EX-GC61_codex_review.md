# Independent review of EX-GC61

**GC61 demonstrates that the calibrated spin probe still works when a locally updated shift replaces the static shift solve.** With the spin source prescribed, the pre-reflection axial spin rate is 1.0012 times the free-space weak-field prediction, and the total-angular-momentum rate is 1.0072 times that prediction. The diagonal spin response also transfers from GC60 without refitting. These are substantive results for the weak, nearly steady field behind the switch-on pulse. The arrival-speed test failed and the off-symmetry total-angular-momentum result limits the gyroscope claim. Six of ten registered predictions passed. C1–C3 passed and C4 was report-only. ## What the local links establish

Neighboring links exchange increments through a prescribed wave equation. With flat weights the event implementation converges toward the same equation integrated by RK4, with a fourfold reduction in their difference when the time step halves. Random reordering works **within each update class**. The drift and kick classes still follow a specified sequence. At time 50 the shell field differs by 0.22% in relative RMS from the larger finite-box static reference. The early detector rates agree between the two dynamic box sizes. Together these support the boundary explanation of most of GC59's axial deficit. The reference uses the same source and coupling, and the main experiment uses unit propagation weights even though the source lapse varies. The weighted variant measured the field only. Neither the coupling strength nor a transport law arising from entanglement was derived. [Implementation](../src/exgc61.py) · [Results B97](../results/EX-GC61/EX-GC61_B97.json)

**Arrival is the most important qualification.** The plan calls for half-rise times of shift components. The actual recorder takes half the local curl with a minus sign, then times its half-rise relative to its own value at time 50. A curl is a sensible axial observable where symmetry can suppress the shift itself, but changing the observable changes the waveform. Its fitted time-versus-distance slopes were 0.89 axially and 0.83 in the plane. Two boxes agreeing before a reflection tests sensitivity to their walls. It does not independently establish a propagation speed equal to the model's light speed. Source extent, pulse shape and lattice dispersion remain separable hypotheses. The retarded point-dipole comparison is a partial post hoc explanation, especially weak in the plane. ## Independent checks on the detector

I refitted the source-odd angle from the saved up/down component histories. In the early axial window, the J slopes in the first and second halves were 3.1585 and 3.1928 × 10⁻⁵, consistent with the reported full-window 3.1760 × 10⁻⁵. In the late window the two slopes were **+1.9267 × 10⁻⁵ and −4.2389 × 10⁻⁶**. The advertised 80% reduction averages a transient that actually reverses direction. The late run starts a fresh probe, so it compares detector responses at two field epochs. A late larger-box detector run would directly isolate the reflected contribution. [Spin-up traces](../results/EX-GC61/EX-GC61_C97up.json) · [Spin-down traces](../results/EX-GC61/EX-GC61_C97dn.json)

P5 uses the GC59 **spin** rate as the static denominator for both spin and J. Its quoted J ratio of 1.0309 is consequently a cross-observable comparison. Dividing the same dynamic J rate by the saved GC60 static **J** rate for the same axis site gives **1.0246**. The like-for-like comparison still passes the numerical gate. The free-space comparison is unaffected. At the diagonal, the frozen two-coefficient prediction works for spin (measured/predicted 1.0002), while rotation alone already predicts J to about 0.045%. [Scoring code](../src/exgc61_summary.py) · [Static J data](../results/EX-GC60/EX-GC60_C.json)

**The 41.2% tilted-J discrepancy matters.** The spin vector misses by only 0.9% at the same site, and both observables work in the uniform-gradient control. The failed source-site test used the **static** source field. Timing or reflection of the new travelling field cannot explain that failure. J includes orbital angular momentum about the probe-box origin. A packet that moves can carry an orbital contribution about that origin as well as internal rotation. Before adding another coefficient, compare J about the fixed origin with J about the instantaneous packet center, and compare the source field with an affine field having its same local gradient and lapse conditions. This separates bulk motion, variation across the packet and possible lattice anisotropy. [Vector tests](../src/exgc61.py#L344-L351) · [Scored vector components](../results/EX-GC61/summary.json)

**The matched-detector correction is persuasive within its scope.** The registered preparation gives four failed P8 subtests. Transforming both the initial state and detector post hoc reduces the maximum pure-gauge spin-angle residual to 1.8 × 10⁻⁹ of the shear signal at the tested width and strength. This supports GC60's coordinate-readout interpretation there. The corrected doubled-strength residual ratio is 7.53, outside the planned 3–5 interval and measured on a very small residual. An order-of-error claim needs a strength sweep above the numerical floor. The larger probe box independently suppresses the links-only versus full shear discrepancy as predicted (P9). ## What to do next

1. **Measure transport independently of the gyroscope.** Launch a compact, controlled pulse and record both shift and curl, alongside a light pulse, at several axial and oblique distances. Register the waveform feature, its speed prediction for the lattice bandwidth, and a resolution test in advance. Repeat with the lapse weights enabled. This directly tests the common-speed claim that P2 left open.
2. **Test a conserving source before interpreting radiation.** Start from consistent initial fields and transfer angular momentum between two localized matter regions with a known total. Track matter, field, and boundary flux through the exchange. Then test the delayed probe response and reciprocal torque with the old normalization frozen. Post-ramp field-energy stability under a prescribed current does not demonstrate conservation of the combined matter and field.
3. **Retain the axial probe as a benchmark and diagnose tilted J** using the fixed-center versus packet-center comparison above. Preserve the small field cutouts or make the scorer accept archived gradient matrices. At the reviewed revision it loads intermediate NPY cutouts absent from the tracked results, even though summary numbers and detector histories are available.
