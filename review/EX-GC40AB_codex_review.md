# Independent review of EX-GC40A/B

2026-11-26 | Codex

**GC40A gives a clear negative result for the tested route to a Newton constant. GC40B gives a positive result for deterministic signal propagation through a prescribed gravitational profile.** I would prioritize fixing GC40B’s bending measurement, then test whether the result survives when neighboring links share the same site clocks. Keep the proposed GC40C hub experiment small and use it to distinguish a local defect from a source of a long-range field.

## What Chain Theory contributes

A. D. Wissner-Gross’s *Chain Theory: Toward Fundamental Physics from Distilling Networks of Entangled Qudits* (October 2026 working draft) starts from maximally entangled qudit pairs, some qudits shared between pairs, and access through distillation (Definition 1.1). GC40A implements the pair vacuum using distinct fermion modes at each link end, then couples the modes at a site. This models how entanglement spreads beyond pairs through a different construction from the compendium’s shared-qudit and distillation rules. In GC40B, adjacency and firing rates fully determine propagation.

The compendium’s count-level field equation balances in **0 of 60** tested cases (Open Result 92.1), and its hub-to-mass results are conditional (Theorems 185.6–185.9). GC40 and Chain Theory expose a similar gap between counting and a gravitational source law, through different calculations. [GC40 plan](../spec/EX-GC40_plan.md)

## GC40A — Entanglement and geometry

**The geometry measurement works within its tested scope.** The exact ln 2 per cut link in the undressed vacuum is an implementation check. After dressing, removing two cut links with a degree-preserving rewire costs almost the same entropy at faces, edges, and corners. The response is within about 3% of the average entropy per cut link at the tested gapped couplings. The registered prediction fails over its full scope because the gapless case deviates by about 15%. This establishes a useful boundary-area measure for the tested rewires. A relation between those counts and spatial curvature remains to be demonstrated. [GC40A results](../results/EX-GC40A/EX-GC40A_note.md)

**The effective G runs strongly with block size.** It falls by a factor of 2.3–3.0 when the cube side doubles from 3 to 6 sites, against a 20% tolerance. The central differences are stable across the two step sizes tested, and the independent modular-Hamiltonian calculation agrees on the checked small blocks. The registered linearity check failed, but that failure gives little reason to expect the size drift to disappear. I would close the claim that this vacuum, matter perturbation, and calibration yield a constant G. Jacobson’s continuum argument concerns small geodesic balls and a specific modular-energy response. The lattice cubes, short range of sizes, and single family of coherent perturbations make GC40A a limited test of that broader argument. [GC40A source](../src/exgc40a.py) · [Jacobson, *Entanglement Equilibrium and the Einstein Equation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04753)

**The pure-pair result needs narrower wording.** At zero dressing, both the first-order entropy change and the first-order energy-density change vanish for the chosen perturbation. The ratio used to infer G is undefined. The test establishes first-order entropy stationarity along that perturbation. It leaves open how a pure-pair vacuum responds to an excitation that carries energy at the leading order being measured. [GC40A results](../results/EX-GC40A/EX-GC40A_note.md)

## GC40B — Event counting and light propagation

**The deterministic result shows that the coincidence rule survives irregular connectivity.** The speed exponent of 1.996 is largely built into the rule. For a short coincidence window τ, the hop rate is approximately 2τNᵢNⱼ. Uniform N rescales nearly every travel time by the same inverse-square factor. The spatially varying test gives a normalized deterministic delay ratio of **2.004** at b = 60, with **2.019** and **1.990** in the two graph halves. The absolute two-site delay is about **93% of the straight-path continuum estimate** there. A matched finite-endpoint continuum travel time would help establish whether the remaining difference comes from the graph or the benchmark. [GC40B results](../results/EX-GC40B/EX-GC40B_note.md) · [Deterministic lens data](../results/EX-GC40B/lens_det.json)

**Bending and isotropy remain unresolved.** The registered bending test failed, and its benchmark was miscalibrated. At b = 40 and 60, the continuum calculation for the actual endpoints predicts ratios of **1.77** and **1.87**, respectively. The path-angle estimates are noisy enough that the near-perfect absolute match at b = 60 carries little weight on its own. The isotropy test also missed its 1% gate. The spread is consistent with estimated sampling noise, so the experiment lacks the precision to decide isotropy at 1%. [GC40B results](../results/EX-GC40B/EX-GC40B_note.md)

**The stochastic result tests independent random edge weights.** The code draws one crossing time per directed edge and then runs shortest path on those fixed draws. In a network of site clocks, one firing can affect several edges, and the wait for a crossing depends on when the signal becomes eligible to cross. The implementation omits those correlations and uses separate draws for the flat and lens cases. The noisy stochastic ratios leave the shared-clock mechanism open. [GC40B source](../src/exgc40b.py)

## New tests, in priority order

1. **Measure bending from the arrival-time front.** Use the existing graph ensemble for a pilot. For the scored run, sample a broad transverse receiver array and estimate the slope of the arrival-time front, with a paired flat run on each graph. Apply the same slope estimator to the continuum solution at the same source and receiver locations. Score absolute delay and absolute deflection for each rule before taking their ratio. Use graph-level confidence intervals and set the ensemble size from the pilot variance. Add a flat null and a weaker lens to check that the estimator recovers zero and the expected weak-field trend.

2. **Run a shared-clock simulation on smaller graphs.** Give every site one Poisson event stream and let all incident links use its actual history. Pair the flat and lens simulations using the same underlying random draws. Compare deterministic travel times, independent-edge first passage, and shared-clock first passage on the same graphs. If studying a collective signal, define its arrival before the run, for example by a fixed quantile of repeated pulse arrivals. This would establish whether independent edge sampling caused the stochastic discrepancy and whether a reproducible front survives the microscopic rule.

3. **Run GC40C as a hub-versus-defect test.** A local change in the dressing changes the Hamiltonian, so measure the ground-state energy shift and any bound excitation separately. Compare a hub in the pure-pair background, a hub in a dressed background, and a local excitation of the unchanged dressed Hamiltonian. In the pure-pair case, the hub couples only a finite set of dimers, giving a locality control. In the dressed case, measure the response outside the source as box size and source strength vary. Compare short-range decay against a 1/r profile in 3D, and measure the response at a second probe. A reproducible, energy-dependent far field would justify a source-law experiment.

4. **Give GC40A one targeted continuum check.** Use approximately spherical regions and a perturbation with a measurable leading-order energy change. Compare the entropy response directly against the boost-weighted energy integral, across sizes and at least two distinct source profiles. Establish a range where the lattice spacing is much smaller than the region and the region is much smaller than the source wavelength and box size. Defer larger production runs until the pilot shows that such a range exists.

The next decision point is whether GC40B can recover both delay and bending with one propagation rule and one flat calibration, then retain them with shared clocks. For emergent gravity, the remaining question is whether the same microscopic model can predict the lapse from a localized energy source. GC40A’s entropy and GC40B’s lapse are currently calculated separately, with no equation connecting them.
