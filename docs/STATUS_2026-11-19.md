# Model Gc: where the investigation stands (2026-11-19)

Written by Claude at the PI's request ("write this up as a summary at this point in the investigation and commit").
This is a summary of the existing records and introduces no new results. Every study except MG195 is **EXPLORATORY**
(see `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`); exploratory results support no claims on their own. Model Gc, and every variant and
rule below, is our construction, not LaViolette's.

## In one paragraph
Model Gc gives particles real long-range gravity. Each particle is a steady reaction–diffusion structure whose core
consumes a conserved species G, so it is surrounded by a 1/r deficit of G. Particles drift toward lower G, so pairs
attract as 1/d². This has been confirmed directly and in one registered study (MG195). The equivalence principle is the
weak point: free fall depends on particle type and on binding. Two attempts at spin-like behaviour from the chemistry
alone (rotation, an internal clock) failed. A structural route does work in principle: ribbon frames on the graph
plus a local "no-cut" rule, with the particle's own G supply as the tether. There, a particle twisted by one turn keeps
a twist that costs it G consumption, while two turns undo it. The exchange (fermion) sign and any link to quantum
mechanics are still open.

## 1. Gravity: solid
| Study | Question | Result | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| EX008 | Can a particle make a true long-range G field? | Yes. With G conserved and consumed only in the core, G = G∞ − M/(4πr) to 1e-6, where M is the consumption. Linear response says particles drift toward lower G, so pairs attract as 1/d². | `results/EX008_longrange/EX008_note.md` |
| EX009 | Direct check of the pair force | A 2D axisymmetric pair solve gives 0.984–0.986 of the 1/d² prediction at d = 20–30, with Turing repulsion at short range (d ≈ 8). The 1/r range holds down to k = 0.001. | `results/EX009_followups/EX009_note.md` |
| EX010 | Charge plus gravity | Tuned mirror charges (exactly opposite Q and μ) give like-repel/unlike-attract. With G added, every type drifts toward lower G, and mobilities agree within 1–2%. | `results/EX010_charge_gravity/EX010_note.md` |
| **MG195** (confirmatory, PI closed) | Inverse-square law | P1 and P2 SUPPORTED (v/prediction 0.98–0.99, exponent 1.98). C2 (Yukawa control) PASS. P3 UNRESOLVED: a short-range Turing tail set the null floor too high. | `results/MG195_inverse_square/MG195_results.md` |
| EX-GC09 | Does it survive discreteness? | On a 3D lattice graph (h = 0.5), as whole tokens, the far G deficit matches the lattice point-sink field within 1–4%. | `results/EX-GC09/EX-GC09_note.md` |

Known gaps (`docs/PHYSICS_MAPPING.md`): the model is overdamped (no built-in inertia), G propagates by diffusion
(not as a wave), and the G budget is one-sided (consumption with a far reservoir).

## 2. Equivalence principle: the weak point
| Study | Question | Result | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| EX-GC01 | Action and reaction for unequal particles | v_i = μ_i M_j/(4πd²) within 1–2%. M_a v_a = M_b v_b only when μ_a = μ_b, so free fall is not universal across types. | `results/EX-GC01/EX-GC01_note.md` |
| EX-GC02 | Inertia from slow species | A slow core gives a real mass (drag linear in the response time, with a Jordan-chain mass), but the threshold depends on type (17.5–40), so free fall is still not universal. | `results/EX-GC02/EX-GC02_note.md` |
| EX-GC03 | Coupling design for universal free fall | Active and passive gravitational mass agree within 5%, but drag is set by the shared core, so fall speeds spread by 35%. Feeding G to W gives μ = 0 exactly. A tuned slow G gets the spread to 4–5%, but only by tuning. | `results/EX-GC03/EX-GC03_note.md` |
| EX-GC04 | Do composites of identical particles fall alike? | An identical pair binds at d = 7.145 (and 10.11), falls at 0.939 (0.972) of a single particle's speed, and consumes 0.954 of 2M. The cause is G-dependent consumption (dM/dG = 4.58). Binding breaks the equivalence principle. | `results/EX-GC04/EX-GC04_note.md` |

Design target still open: one quantity must set both G consumption and inertia, or binding must not change
consumption.

## 3. Spin, route 1 (rotation plus an internal clock): did not work
| Study | Question | Result | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| EX-GC05 | Does any non-round mode start rotating by itself? | No. Core modes l = 0–3 stay real (non-oscillating) across the parameter range; an ether instability comes first. | `results/EX-GC05/EX-GC05_note.md` |
| EX-GC06 | A core with its own clock (slow inhibitor V, "Gc3") | It gives a clock, but a breathing (l = 0) one, with period about 18–20. l = 1 oscillates only later, l = 2 and 3 never, and there is no particle for κ ≥ 1.1. | `results/EX-GC06/EX-GC06_note.md` |

Each fix in this direction adds another tuned ingredient. Route 2 (an orientation field) was set aside by the PI as
unnatural, since it is unclear what would create a twisted field. Route 3 was chosen instead.

## 4. Spin, route 3 (ribbons in the graph, tethered by the G supply): works in principle
Design: `docs/ROUTE3_RIBBON_DESIGN.md`.

| Study | Question | Result | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| EX-GC08 (Stage 1) | Can local graph rules reproduce the 720° (belt-trick) topology? | Yes, with a three-node no-cut rule: short SU(2) lifts must compose. That gives H₁ = Z₂ and a conserved index. One turn sticks (about 530 mismatched edges), two turns untangle to 0. Pairwise rules either leak or freeze. | `results/EX-GC08/EX-GC08_note.md` |
| EX-GC09 (Stage 2) | Can the Gc particle live as tokens on that lattice graph? | Yes. Deterministically, M is 5.76 (h = 0.5) or 5.20 (h = 0.4), against 5.32 radial. As tokens it survives at Ω = 1000 (M −6%) and 300 (−20%) to T = 30, fades at 100, and dies at 30, so it needs about 10⁴ core tokens. A splitting bias in the first token code was found and fixed. | `results/EX-GC09/EX-GC09_note.md` |
| EX-GC10 (Stage 3) | Does the particle's own G supply act as a 720° tether? | Yes, in the deterministic runs. One turn: the twist collects just outside the core, the index stays −1 and M is 7.9% lower (3 of 3 seeds). Two turns: it untangles, with M back within 0.6%. Controls: with ε = 0 the twist sticks but M is unchanged; under the pairwise rule the twist leaks. Token runs (Ω = 3000) agree on the topology, but their mass signal is noisy (a shared lattice-drift artefact). | `results/EX-GC10/EX-GC10_note.md` |

Caveats for route 3:
- The coupling strength ε and the frame temperature are chosen, so the size of the mass shift is not a prediction.
- The twist is imposed by hand.
- A two-turn tether often keeps a small leftover tangle that is not topologically protected.

## 5. Open questions, roughly in order
1. **Exchange sign.** Does swapping two tethered particles match a 360° turn of one (the classical spin–statistics
   link, the fermion signature)? This would be route 3, Stage 3b.
2. **A real rotation.** A particle that rotates relative to its tether by itself, rather than being twisted by hand.
3. **Universal free fall.** A coupling where inertia and G consumption come from the same quantity, and binding does
   not break it.
4. **Discrete robustness.** Lattice convergence (M differs by 10% between h = 0.5 and 0.4), longer token runs, and
   the survival threshold in Ω. The parent ModelG study found Ω ≳ 3000 was needed for long-lived Model G particles.
5. **Link to quantum mechanics.** Whether the classical tether sign could become a quantum sign, and causal
   invariance of the rule set (the parent programme's goal).
