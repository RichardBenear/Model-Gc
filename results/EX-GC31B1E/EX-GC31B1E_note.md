## Native two-ended replacement rule
Finite connected simple seed of degree 1..3. An active vertex has one incidence token for each bond. Vertex event consumes its incidences and retires itself; creates two daughters per old port, one for each shared bond lane tag 0/1. Within the daughters, the following symmetric connected gadgets are added: d=1, an edge; d=2, K2,2 between lane sets; d=3, K3,3 excluding same-old-port pairings (six-cycle). Internal bonds produce their two ready incidence tokens. Each daughter has one reserved outside port, ready only when the old bond has received both offers. Each old bond owns exactly two endpoint offer slots. Its event consumes both offers, retires itself, and creates two new bonds joining corresponding lane daughters on opposite ends, producing their ready incidence tokens. This is a local two-phase handshake, not a global step. Lane tags are part of the rule's matching protocol, not emergent physical directions; simultaneous consistent relabel of an inherited bond's two lanes is immaterial to cross matching, though the whole gadget has only its stated symmetries. No claim unrestricted unlabeled replacements.

## Proof
Distinct enabled vertex events consume disjoint endpoint tokens even when they share bond; they write separate offer slots, retire distinct owners and create unique daughter/internal-bond identities. They commute. Distinct enabled bond events consume disjoint offers, create distinct bonds/incidences, only append ready tokens into separate reserved slots; commute even if daughters shared (in this gadget each daughter has only one external reservation). A bond event requires retirement of its two old endpoint owners, so cannot compete with those old vertex events; each recipient daughter must wait for its ready port (can't retire before delivery). Other ready vertex/bond events touch independent slots; their outputs and ancestry commute. Unique events per vertex/bond instance; no competing claimant to an input. This is not a proof for arbitrary destructive matching.

Define local descendant generation g for each vertex/bond (initial 0). A vertex firing g creates daughters and internal bonds at g+1; a bond firing g consumes its two g offers and produces its two g+1 bonds. Daughters need all their incidence tokens before any further action. Finite cut R completes vertex and bond events of g<R; dependency graph stratifies by generation and within each generation vertex→bond, so finite and acyclic, with readiness stable and every missing input supplied from registered lower stratum. By induction over strata, any fair execution completes the same event set, including normalized consumed identities and producer/consumer DAG.
